Buying guide

Best CRM for small business

The best CRM is the one your team actually updates. Here is how the main options differ, and the adoption problem that decides whether any of them works.

By Alexander Pace·Published July 2, 2026·Updated August 20, 2026·9 min read

How this guide was made

Research-based analysis of published pricing, documentation and feature tiers, informed by our own experience running a small sales pipeline. It is not a hands-on comparison of all five platforms at scale. See our editorial policy.

Key takeaways

  • Adoption beats features. A simple CRM your team updates daily is worth more than a powerful one they avoid.
  • Free tiers are real and useful. The upgrade cliff is where the cost is — check the price of the tier you will need in 18 months, not the one you start on.
  • Contact-based pricing (HubSpot) and seat-based pricing (Pipedrive, Zoho) scale very differently. Model both against your actual growth.
  • Most CRM failures are process failures: no agreed definition of a stage, no data hygiene, and no reason for a salesperson to log anything.

Decide what you are actually buying it for

CRM is a broad category, and the products differ most in which of these they are built around:

  • Pipeline management. Tracking deals through stages, forecasting, activity. The core use for most small B2B teams.
  • Marketing automation. Email sequences, landing pages, lead scoring, attribution.
  • Customer support. Ticketing and service history.
  • Operations hub. The system of record everything else integrates with.

If you only need the first, buying a platform built for all four means paying for and navigating a great deal you will not use — which is itself a leading cause of poor adoption.

The platforms

Indicative list pricing as of August 2026. Annual billing typically saves 15–20%, and this category discounts heavily at the end of a quarter.

1

HubSpot

Best free tier, and best if you need marketing too

A genuinely capable free CRM with unlimited users, sitting under a marketing and service platform you can grow into — at a price that rises steeply when you do.

Pricing
Free tier with unlimited users; paid Sales Hub from around $20 per seat per month; Professional tiers jump substantially
Best for
Teams that want marketing and sales in one place, and anyone starting from spreadsheets

The free tier is not a crippled trial. Unlimited users, contact and deal management, email tracking, meeting scheduling and basic reporting are all included, and for a small team that is often sufficient for a year or more. Onboarding is the smoothest in the category.

The commercial model is the thing to understand before committing. Pricing is driven by marketing contacts, so your bill grows with your database rather than your team, and the step from Starter to Professional is a large one — frequently several times the price. Companies routinely find themselves needing one Professional-only feature and facing a bill they did not plan for. Model the Professional price now, not later.

Strengths

  • Best free tier available, with unlimited users
  • Excellent onboarding and documentation
  • Marketing, sales and service genuinely integrated
  • Very large integration ecosystem

Trade-offs

  • Steep price jump from Starter to Professional
  • Contact-based pricing scales with your database, not your team
  • More platform than a pipeline-only team needs
  • Annual contracts on paid tiers
2

Pipedrive

Best pure pipeline CRM

Built around a visual sales pipeline and little else, which is exactly why sales teams actually use it.

Pricing
From around $14 per seat per month; most teams land on a mid tier around $34–$49
Best for
Small B2B sales teams whose problem is deal tracking, not marketing

Pipedrive made a deliberate decision to be a sales tool rather than a platform, and the focus shows. The drag-and-drop pipeline is immediately comprehensible, data entry is minimal, and a new salesperson is productive in an afternoon. Given that adoption is the main determinant of CRM success, that simplicity is a feature rather than a limitation.

It is less suited to organisations wanting marketing automation, complex service workflows or heavy customisation. Several useful capabilities — notably email sequencing and lead scoring — sit behind higher tiers or paid add-ons, so price the tier you need rather than the entry point.

Strengths

  • Fastest to adopt of the major options
  • Clear, visual pipeline that salespeople like using
  • Transparent per-seat pricing
  • Strong mobile app for field sales

Trade-offs

  • Limited marketing automation
  • Key features gated to higher tiers or add-ons
  • Reporting is thinner than HubSpot or Zoho
3

Zoho CRM

Best value at scale

Enterprise-grade capability at a fraction of the price, particularly compelling as part of the wider Zoho One bundle.

Pricing
From around $14 per user per month; Zoho One bundles 40+ applications for roughly $37–$45 per employee
Best for
Cost-conscious teams needing depth, and businesses wanting a full software suite

Zoho’s value proposition is difficult to argue with on price. The CRM includes workflow automation, custom modules, forecasting and analytics that competitors reserve for tiers costing several times more. Zoho One goes further, bundling accounting, projects, help desk, email and dozens of other applications at a per-employee price that undercuts buying two or three point solutions.

The trade-off is polish and coherence. The interface is denser and less refined, the applications in the suite vary in quality, and configuration takes longer. Teams that value speed of adoption over breadth often prefer Pipedrive despite paying more.

Strengths

  • Exceptional functionality per dollar
  • Deep customisation and workflow automation
  • Zoho One is remarkable value for a full stack
  • Strong reporting and forecasting at low tiers

Trade-offs

  • Interface is less polished and more cluttered
  • Longer setup and configuration time
  • Quality varies across the wider suite
  • Support experience is inconsistent on lower tiers
4

Salesforce Starter / Pro Suite

Best if you will genuinely outgrow everything else

The industry standard, with entry-level bundles aimed at small businesses — worth considering mainly if you expect to need Salesforce eventually.

Pricing
Starter from around $25 per user per month; full Sales Cloud considerably more
Best for
Fast-growing companies who will need enterprise CRM within a few years

Salesforce is the most capable and most extensible CRM in existence, with an unmatched partner ecosystem and a labour market full of people who know it. If you are confident you will need that within two or three years, starting there avoids a painful migration at exactly the moment you are busiest.

For most small businesses, though, it remains more than is needed. It expects configuration, benefits from an administrator, and its total cost — licences plus implementation plus the add-ons that turn out to be necessary — is meaningfully higher than the alternatives. Buying it "because it is the standard" is how small teams end up with an expensive, half-configured system nobody uses.

Strengths

  • Most extensible platform, with the largest app ecosystem
  • Abundant expertise available to hire or contract
  • Scales to essentially any size
  • Deep reporting and forecasting

Trade-offs

  • Overkill for most small businesses
  • Realistically needs an administrator
  • Add-ons push the true cost well above list
  • Slowest to implement of the options here
5

A spreadsheet, honestly

Fine below about five deals a week

For a very small pipeline, a well-structured spreadsheet is faster to maintain and easier to change than any CRM.

Pricing
Free
Best for
Solo founders and teams closing a handful of deals a month

A CRM earns its place when you cannot hold the pipeline in your head, when more than one person needs the same view, or when follow-ups are being missed. Below that, a spreadsheet with columns for company, contact, stage, value, next action and next action date does the job with no licence and no configuration.

The point at which to switch is usually clear: when you start forgetting follow-ups, when a second salesperson joins, or when you need history rather than current state. Migrating a clean spreadsheet into a CRM is straightforward; migrating three years of messy CRM data is not — so keep the spreadsheet tidy in the meantime.

Strengths

  • Free, instant, and infinitely flexible
  • Zero adoption problem for one person
  • Trivial to export into a real CRM later

Trade-offs

  • No automation, reminders or email tracking
  • Breaks down with more than one user
  • No audit trail or history

Why most CRM implementations fail

It is almost never the software. Four failure modes account for the overwhelming majority:

  1. Nobody updates it. If logging activity costs a salesperson time and returns them nothing, they will not do it. The CRM must give something back — reminders that prevent lost deals, context before a call, one less report to write.
  2. Stages mean different things to different people. If "qualified" is undefined, your pipeline is fiction and so is your forecast. Write down the exit criteria for each stage before configuring anything.
  3. Migrating dirty data. Importing three years of duplicated, half-complete spreadsheet rows destroys trust in the system on day one. Clean first, or import only what is active.
  4. Configured for the manager, not the seller. A CRM designed around the reports a director wants, with fifteen required fields, is one salespeople will fight. Ask what the person entering data gets out of each field.

Trial with real deals, not sample data

Run your actual pipeline in two candidates for two weeks. Sample data hides exactly the friction that determines adoption. Ask the person who will use it most which one they would rather open every morning — that answer predicts success better than any feature matrix.

Before you commit

  • Check the export path. Can you get your data out in a usable format, including notes, activity history and attachments? Ask before signing, not at renewal.
  • Model the tier you will need in 18 months. Not the one you start on. The upgrade cliff is where CRM budgets break.
  • Verify the integrations you depend on — email, calendar, accounting, support — are on your tier. Several are commonly gated to higher plans.
  • Check API limits if you plan to build anything against it. They vary substantially and are rarely prominent.
  • Confirm what happens if you downgrade. Some platforms make data read-only or inaccessible below a certain tier.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free CRM for a small business?

HubSpot’s free tier is the most capable, with unlimited users, contact and deal management, email tracking and meeting scheduling. Zoho offers a free tier limited to three users. For a small team starting from spreadsheets, HubSpot free is usually the right first step — just check the price of the paid tier you will eventually need.

How much should a small business spend on CRM?

Most small teams land between $15 and $50 per user per month. A five-person sales team should budget roughly $1,000–$3,000 a year. Beyond licences, allow for implementation time — someone has to configure it and clean the data, and that is usually the larger cost in the first year.

HubSpot or Pipedrive?

Pipedrive if your problem is tracking deals and you want the fastest adoption. HubSpot if you also need marketing automation, or if the free tier’s unlimited users matters more than simplicity. Pipedrive is the better pure sales tool; HubSpot is the better platform.

Do I need a CRM at all?

Not if you are a solo operator closing a handful of deals a month and nothing is falling through the cracks. You need one when follow-ups start being missed, when more than one person needs the same view of a customer, or when you need history rather than just current state.

How long does CRM implementation take?

A simple Pipedrive or HubSpot setup can be running in a day. A properly configured system with cleaned data, defined stages, integrations and trained users is more like two to six weeks. Salesforce with real customisation is a project measured in months.

Can I move my data if I switch later?

Contacts, companies and deals export cleanly from all the major platforms. What travels badly is activity history, notes, attachments and custom-field structure — so check the export format before committing, and keep in mind that the longer you stay, the more expensive leaving becomes.
